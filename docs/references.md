# References and further reading

These sources provide technical background. The four-stage framework, timelines and philosophical interpretations are an editorial thesis, not claims established by the papers.

- Vaswani et al. (2017). [*Attention Is All You Need*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). Transformer architecture and attention-based sequence modeling.
- Kaplan et al. (2020). [*Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361). Empirical relationships among loss, compute, data and model size.
- Hoffmann et al. (2022). [*Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). Model–data scaling tradeoffs.
- Dao et al. (2022). [*FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135). Tiled attention and memory traffic.
- Brown et al. (2020). [*Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). In-context task generalization.
- Yao et al. (2022). [*ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629). Interleaving reasoning and tool actions.

## A note on uncertainty

Forecasts about agentic computing, business-model disruption, quantum computing and a scientific renaissance remain speculative. They should be interpreted as possible developments, not inevitable outcomes.
