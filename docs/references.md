# References and Further Reading

This page collects the technical and scientific references behind **The Four Ages of AI: From Machine Intelligence to Human Reinvention**. They are grouped by the ideas discussed in the essay, so readers can trace the evidence and explore the research in greater depth.

The four-age framework, its interpretation of economic and human consequences, and the proposed fourth age are **the author's synthesis and hypotheses**, not findings established by any single source.

## Age 1 — Architecture and the Transformer transition

1. **Vaswani et al. (2017).** [*Attention Is All You Need*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). Introduced the Transformer; foundational to the essay's account of architectural convergence and parallelizable sequence modeling.
2. **Sutskever, Vinyals & Le (2014).** [*Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3215). Illustrates recurrent encoder–decoder sequence learning preceding the Transformer.
3. **He et al. (2015).** [*Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385). An influential example of specialized deep learning architecture in computer vision.
4. **Goodfellow et al. (2014).** [*Generative Adversarial Nets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661). A representative generative modeling approach from the architecturally diverse era.

## Age 2 — LLMs, scaling and parallel computing

5. **Brown et al. (2020).** [*Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). In-context learning and adaptation without conventional per-task training.
6. **Kaplan et al. (2020).** [*Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361). Empirical relationships among language modeling performance, parameters, data and training compute.
7. **Hoffmann et al. (2022).** [*Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). Reexamines how model parameters and training tokens should scale under a fixed compute budget.
8. **Dao et al. (2022).** [*FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135). Central to the essay's discussion of tiling, block-oriented computation and GPU memory reads and writes.
9. **Shazeer et al. (2017).** [*Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538). Foundational work on conditional computation and sparse expert activation.
10. **Yao et al. (2022).** [*ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629). Research on interleaving reasoning traces and tool actions.

## Age 3 — Agentic systems, tools and reliability

11. **Schick et al. (2023).** [*Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761). A research example of language models selecting and using external tools.
12. **Shinn et al. (2023).** [*Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366). Explores feedback, memory and improved action trajectories.
13. **NIST (2023).** [*Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1). A framework for governance and management of AI-related risk.
14. **OWASP.** [*Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications*](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/). Practical security risks including prompt injection, inappropriate tool access and related application-layer challenges.

These references illuminate agentic capabilities and the need for governance. They do **not** independently prove the essay's forecasts about SaaS disruption, personal hardware or future economic institutions.

## Age 4 — AI for science, mathematics and quantum computation

15. **Romera-Paredes et al. (2023).** [*Mathematical Discoveries from Program Search with Large Language Models*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06924-6). FunSearch; illustrates LLM-guided discovery coupled with automated evaluation.
16. **Trinh et al. (2024).** [*Solving Olympiad Geometry without Human Demonstrations*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06747-5). AlphaGeometry; a system combining neural methods and symbolic deduction.
17. **Jumper et al. (2021).** [*Highly Accurate Protein Structure Prediction with AlphaFold*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2). A landmark example of machine learning enabling scientific prediction.
18. **Preskill (2018).** [*Quantum Computing in the NISQ Era and Beyond*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00862). Explains opportunities and limitations of near-term quantum computation, and why fault tolerance matters.

These works are evidence of progress in AI-assisted scientific computation and mathematical problem-solving, **not evidence that a revolution in fundamental theoretical physics will necessarily occur within 20 years**.
